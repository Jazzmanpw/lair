# Building Mode Content Authoring Guidance Draft

This draft collects content-shaping rules discovered while prototyping Running Mode. They belong to Building Mode because authoring should help the GM produce material that remains readable and useful during play.

The document is an accumulator, not a finished authoring UX specification. Future Building Mode work should decide whether each rule becomes validation, a soft warning, an example, or quiet structural guidance.

## Scene Details

Scene details describe only facts the PCs can notice without checks.

They establish the shared physical situation the GM needs to keep in working memory and can communicate immediately. Information that requires Perception, Recall Knowledge, investigation, or another check belongs with an inquiry reference instead.

Prefer a short bullet list that can be scanned as one coherent unit. Room shape, dimensions, and passages may move into an optional scene sketch if that direction survives its own prototype.

Scene details can link to deeper free-form prep notes when a visible fact needs additional context or mechanics. Keep the detail focused on what the PCs can notice and let the referenced note own the procedure.

## Inquiry References

An inquiry reference describes facts the PCs can discover through a particular information-seeking action and check.

In the current vault, the compact identity is usually the in-fiction inquiry action and its target, such as `Идентифицировать магию о рунах над креслом`. Running Mode item prototypes will decide whether the in-app identity should stay action-first, become target-first, use inquiry-type icons, include roll context, or use another scan shape. Regardless of display shape, the authored note should preserve enough identity to distinguish what the PCs are trying to learn about and what kind of inquiry resolves it.

Its resolution content contains the skill, DC, adjustments, and effects of the roll. The effects primarily describe the facts disclosed at each relevant degree of success.

Inquiry references are an intentionally sparse set of prepared discovery interactions. They record places where the author expects inquiry and chooses to spend creative effort in advance. Authoring and Running Mode must not assume that every possible inquiry has been documented; ordinary checks should remain easy to adjudicate through accessible system references.

Keep deeper descriptions and reusable mechanics in explicitly linked free-form prep notes. Link them from the inquiry reference when the GM needs them to interpret or resolve the check.

Inquiry references are a recurring structured prep-note shape for roll-guarded discovery. Continued adventure prep should test whether that shape stays coherent and whether other recurring prep-note shapes deserve their own runtime treatment.

## Situational Rules

A situational rule is the current name for a free-form prep-note usage that provides additional context or mechanics for something that may happen.

It may describe:

- a special interaction with a place or object;
- a procedure, effect, puzzle, or randomizer;
- details supporting an inquiry reference, hazard, item, creature, or other prepared content;
- deeper context that does not fit a concise structured unit.

Situational rules relate to the content that gives them context; one rule may support several scenes or other prep units. References allow reusable mechanics to remain in appropriately scoped notes instead of being duplicated. A concise scene detail or inquiry reference can link to a situational rule without absorbing its full procedure.

Checks for manipulating, traversing, physically searching, or otherwise changing a situation have not yet shown a stable dedicated runtime format. For now they should remain in free-form procedure notes unless continued real adventure prep reveals a recurring shape worth prototyping. The seek-facts versus interact-with-the-situation distinction is a working content model to test against continued Lab prep.

## Flavor Text

Flavor text is optional player-facing introductory narration.

Keep it to at most five sentences. This is a perceptual budget for reading or paraphrasing an introduction at the table, not merely a display truncation rule.

Flavor text and scene details may overlap in subject because they serve different readers and moments: flavor text introduces the scene to players, while scene details remain the GM's stable factual reference.
